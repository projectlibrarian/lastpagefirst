# Intentional Outcomes: Last Page First

Slides and resources for the **NYLA 2026 Annual Conference pre-conference workshop**, *Intentional Outcomes: A Project Planning Session for Accidental Project Managers*, presented by Ron Kirsop, PMP.

**Open the slides:** `index.html` (once published on GitHub Pages, use the site link on the Project Librarian website).

## What this is

A 3-hour, hands-on workshop that teaches one repeatable method for planning library projects: **Last Page First**. Participants bring a real project (their *Anchor Project*) and build a short plan for it on one folded sheet of paper. The slides double as a reference you can come back to after the session.

The method comes from *Intentional Outcomes: A Project Management Guide for Nonprofits and Local Governments*.

## The method in five steps

| Step                     | Question it answers                                          | Gate: who has to say go?                                 |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| **0. Shelf Check**       | Is this a project?                                           | You                                                      |
| **1. Last Page**         | What does the ending look like, and who can approve it?      | Sponsor approves the Last Page                           |
| **2. Table of Contents** | What are the deliverables, and what will they cost in time and money? | Sponsor agrees to the plan as the baseline               |
| **3. Chapters**          | How do we run it one stage at a time?                        | Sponsor decides at chapter end: continue / adjust / stop |
| **4. Epilogue**          | How do we finish on purpose?                                 | Sponsor accepts in writing                               |

A "gate" is a checkpoint: the named person has to say go before you move on. Projects are sized by scoring five factors (duration, people, money, visibility, reversibility) from 0 to 2: **0-3 Pamphlet, 4-7 Paperback, 8-10 Hardcover.** Every edition keeps the same five steps. Only the amount of writing changes.

## What's in the deck

- Opening conversation and the Quick Test for "is it a project?"
- The Last Page First method map, with a worked example (a Library of Things launch)
- Writing the Last Page, the Stranger Test, and the Pamphlet / Paperback / Hardcover scoring rule
- Work breakdown, the First Edition, scope creep vs. drift vs. gold plating, and four scope curveballs
- The triple constraint, PERT estimates, and using board meetings as milestones
- Stakeholder mapping, the pre-mortem, and risk responses
- Chapters, boards and WIP (work in progress) limits, the weekly check, four-part status reports, and change control
- Closing a project: handoff, lessons learned, archiving, and the closing announcement
- A one-page take-home checklist
- "Problems and fixes" slides at the end of each segment

## Using the slides

- **Keyboard:** right arrow, down arrow, Page Down, or space to advance; left arrow, up arrow, or Page Up to go back; Home and End to jump.
- **Mouse or touch:** click or tap the right edge to advance, the left edge to go back.
- **Deep links:** add `#12` to the URL to open a specific slide (for example, `index.html#12`).
- A percent-complete bar and counter run along the bottom of the screen.
- Works in any modern browser. No build step or installs. The only outside resource is the Inter font from Google Fonts; the deck still works without it.

## Notes

- All names, dates, and dollar figures in the worked example are invented for illustration.
- Method inspired by and aligned with PMI's five process groups, with tools borrowed from agile, lean (minimum viable product), PRINCE2 (managing by stages), and Gary Klein's pre-mortem.
